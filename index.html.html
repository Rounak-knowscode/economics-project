The Strait of Hormuz Crisis 4-5 pages

4.1 Geographic & Strategic Importance (1 page)

  • Location and dimensions (20 miles wide at narrowest point)
  • Historical significance as international waterway
  • Volume of trade: 1/3 of seaborne crude oil, 1/5 of LNG

4.2 The Closure & Iran's Blockade Strategy (1.5 pages)

  • Scale of attacks: 180+ ballistic missiles to Israel (Day 1), 250+ to Gulf states
  • 3,000 merchant vessels stranded (10% of global container fleet)
  • Iran's improvised toll system during the war

4.3 Alternative Export Routes (1.5-2 pages)

  • UAE: Habshan-Fujairah pipeline (bypasses strait)

  • Iraq: Iraq-Turkey pipeline through Kurdistan (600,000 barrels/day potential)
  • Iran: Jask port pipeline (15% of exports)
  • Limitations of pipelines: capacity constraints, political issues
Key Statistics to Include: Global oil production shrank 8% but prices spiked 60% initially (eventually settling around $100/barrel)
Section 5

Oil Market Economics Analysis 5-6 pages

5.1 Supply & Demand Fundamentals (1.5 pages)

  • Pre-war global oil production levels
  • Gulf region's share of global production
  • Actual supply contraction: 8% vs perceived disruption
  • Countries affected: production shutdowns in Iraq, Kuwait, UAE
  • Storage capacity issues forcing production cuts

5.2 Price Elasticity of Demand (2 pages)

  • Define elasticity: relationship between price and quantity demanded
  • Comparison example: Breakfast cereal (elastic: -3.0) vs Oil (inelastic: -0.1)
  • Why oil is inelastic: no immediate substitutes, non-discretionary use
  • Short-term vs long-term elasticity differences
  • Calculate: 8% supply drop → 60% price increase = -0.13 elasticity
  • Real-world implications: consumers cannot quickly reduce consumption

5.3 The Role of Commodity Traders (1.5 pages)

  • How commodity markets work: futures, speculation, physical storage
  • Traders' influence on prices beyond supply/demand
  • Narrative-driven price movements in opening days
  • Market psychology: belief in "limited conflict" initially
  • Self-fulfilling prophecies in commodity trading

5.4 Insurance Markets & Risk Premiums (1 page)

  • War risk insurance cancellations (72-hour notice)
  • Cost explosion: 0.1-0.25% → 3% of ship value per journey
  • Joint War Committee's role in designating high-risk zones
  • Impact on shipping decisions and global trade flows
Include Graphs/Charts: Create visual representations of: (1) Oil price movements over time, (2) Supply/demand curves showing inelasticity, (3) Elasticity comparison table

The Toll System Proposal: Economic & Legal Analysis 5-6 pages

8.1 Iran's Toll Booth Demand (1.5 pages)

  • Proposed fee: $2 million per vessel (~$1 per barrel)
  • Wartime improvisation: cryptocurrency, yuan payments
  • Countries that paid: China, Japan, India, Pakistan, Greece, Malaysia, Egypt
  • Potential revenue: ~$6-14 billion annually for Iran
  • Comparison to government budget (1/7 of operating costs)

8.2 Economic Impact Analysis (2 pages)

  • Bgal think tank calculations: $0.05-0.40 per barrel increase in global prices
  • Who pays: 80% Gulf Arab exporters, 20% rest of world
  • Gulf states' annual cost: $6-14 billion
  • Compare to: Full closure ($50+ billion/month) vs Toll system ($1 billion/month extra)
  • Comparison to other tolls:
  • → Suez Canal: $800,000 per supertanker ($9 billion/year for Egypt = 5% government budget)
  • → Panama Canal: $300,000 per vessel ($5.7 billion/year = 20% government budget)
  • → Hormuz proposal: Could fund 1/7 of Iranian government budget

8.3 International Maritime Law (1.5 pages)

  • UN Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS): freedom of navigation principle
  • Natural straits vs artificial canals distinction
  • Historical precedent: Denmark's Sound Dues (1400s-1857)
  • Turkish Straits (Bosphorus) example: Montreux Convention loopholes
  • Iran's legal justification vs international law

8.4 Geopolitical Power Shift (1 page)

  • Saudi Arabia's "spare capacity" dominance threatened
  • Iran as potential "swing producer" of oil
  • Control over Gulf states' economies
  • Regional hegemony implications
Key Economic Concept: Explain "swing producer" concept - the ability to quickly increase/decrease oil production to influence global prices. Saudi Arabia currently holds 50-60% of global spare capacity.
Section 9

Cascading Global Consequences: Return to Mercantilism? 4-5 pages

9.1 Historical Context: Pre-WWII Trade Systems (1.5 pages)

  • Mercantilism era: tolls, taxes, tributes as norm
  • Examples:
  • → Denmark's Sound Dues (15th-19th century): 2/3 of government revenue
  • → Ottoman/Byzantine Bosphorus tolls
  • → Portuguese "cartas" system in Indian Ocean
  • → Barbary Pirates & Caribbean tributes
  • US founding principle: freedom of navigation (since 1798)

9.2 The Pax Americana System (1.5 pages)

  • Post-WWII: US Navy as global maritime guarantor
  • Economic benefits: free trade, globalization, wealth creation
  • Cost to US: naval operations, freedom of navigation exercises
  • Benefit to world: predictable, low-cost shipping
  • Historical anomaly: 80 years without maritime tolls on major routes

9.3 Domino Effect: Potential Future Tolls (1-1.5 pages)

  • If Hormuz toll is accepted, why not:
  • → Denmark: Sound Dues 2.0 on Baltic oil (60% of Russian exports, $1.3B potential revenue)
  • → Turkey: Higher Bosphorus fees
  • → Bab al-Mandeb: Djibouti, Eritrea, Yemen tolls
  • → Gibraltar: Spain-Morocco cooperation
  • → Straits of Dover: UK-France tolls
  • → Malacca Strait: Indonesia tolls (affects China, Japan, Korea trade)
  • → Northwest Passage: Canada claims (Arctic warming)
  • → Bering Strait: US-Russia future tolls

9.4 Economic Implications of New Mercantilism (0.5-1 page)

  • Increased global trade costs
  • Slower globalization
  • Reduced economic efficiency
  • Naval arms race: countries protecting own trade routes
  • Increased poverty and reduced international cooperation
Critical Analysis Point: This section is where you analyze the long-term systemic risks. Connect economic theory (free trade benefits, transaction costs) to real-world consequences.

David Malpass — World Bank President World Bank Global Economic Prospects, June 2022

"We face a real danger that the world is heading towards a period of prolonged high energy and food prices, with possible stagflation. Developing countries bear the heaviest burden — they have the least capacity to absorb price shocks and the fewest policy tools to respond."

Source: World Bank Global Economic Prospects, June 2022 — directly parallels 2026 Iran conflict fertiliser/food price crisis

Shaktikanta Das — RBI Governor RBI MPC Statement, June 2022

"Crude oil and commodity price shocks originating from geopolitical conflicts transmit rapidly into domestic inflation through fuel, fertiliser, and food price channels. Our estimates suggest that the oil price spike contributed 90–105 basis points to India's headline inflation in 2022."

Source: RBI Monetary Policy Committee Statement, June 2022 — use for India inflation section

Raghuram Rajan — Former RBI Governor, University of Chicago Economic Times Interview, March 2023

"Commodity price shocks from geopolitical conflicts don't resolve quickly. They embed themselves in inflation expectations, force central banks into aggressive tightening, and slow growth simultaneously — the stagflation trap that is hardest to escape from."

Source: Economic Times Interview, March 2023 — use for stagflation analysis section

Haitham Al-Ghais — OPEC Secretary-General OPEC World Oil Outlook 2023

Mean — direct method 1 full page of working

Source: RBI Governor Shaktikanta Das, RBI MPC Statement June 2022

Mean (x̄) = Σ(f × m) ÷ Σf
Step 1: Fill in the frequency table above (already done)
Step 2: Calculate each f × m value:
1–2: 4 × 1.5 = 6.0
2–3: 8 × 2.5 = 20.0
3–4: 12 × 3.5 = 42.0
4–5: 6 × 4.5 = 27.0
Step 3: Sum the column:
Σ(f×m) = 6 + 20 + 42 + 27 = 95.0
Step 4: Apply formula:
Mean = 95.0 ÷ 30
Mean = 3.17 out of 5
Interpretation: The mean rating of 3.17 out of 5 indicates that on average, residents of Betul perceive a moderate-to-significant impact of oil-driven inflation on their daily lives, confirming that Iran conflict-related oil price shocks reach ordinary households in small Indian cities. (Das, RBI 2022)

Median — grouped data formula 1 full page of working

Source: Raghuram Rajan, Economic Times Interview, March 2023

Median = L + [(n/2 – cf) / f] × h

Where: L = lower boundary of median class · n = total frequency · cf = cumulative frequency before median class · f = frequency of median class · h = class width

Step 1: Add cumulative frequency column (done in table above)
Step 2: Find n/2
n/2 = 30/2 = 15
Step 3: Identify median class — where cumulative frequency first exceeds 15:
After class 1–2: cf = 4 (below 15)
After class 2–3: cf = 12 (below 15)
After class 3–4: cf = 24 ✓ (first to exceed 15)
∴ Median class = 3 – 4
Step 4: Identify values:
L = 3 · n/2 = 15 · cf = 12 · f = 12 · h = 1
Step 5: Apply formula:
Median = 3 + [(15 – 12) / 12] × 1
= 3 + [3/12] × 1
= 3 + 0.25
Median = 3.25 out of 5
Interpretation: The median of 3.25 indicates that exactly half of respondents rated the impact above this value — showing that for the majority, oil-driven inflation is felt as a real and significant financial pressure. (Rajan, 2023)

Mode — grouped data formula 1 full page of working

Source: OPEC Secretary-General Haitham Al-Ghais, OPEC World Oil Outlook 2023

Mode = L + [ (f₁ – f₀) / {(f₁ – f₀) + (f₁ – f₂)} ] × h

Where: f₁ = frequency of modal class · f₀ = frequency of class before modal class · f₂ = frequency of class after modal class · L = lower boundary of modal class · h = class width

Step 1: Identify modal class (highest frequency):
Class 3–4 has frequency 12 — highest
∴ Modal class = 3 – 4
Step 2: Identify values:
L = 3 (lower boundary of 3–4)
f₁ = 12 (frequency of modal class 3–4)
f₀ = 8 (frequency of class before: 2–3)
f₂ = 6 (frequency of class after: 4–5)
h = 1 (class width)
Step 3: Apply formula:
Mode = 3 + [(12 – 8) / {(12 – 8) + (12 – 6)}] × 1
= 3 + [4 / (4 + 6)] × 1
= 3 + [4 / 10] × 1
= 3 + 0.4
Mode = 3.4 out of 5
Interpretation: The mode of 3.4 reveals the most common response range — the majority of respondents rated oil price impact between 3 and 4. When compared with mean (3.17) and median (3.25), the close proximity of all three measures (3.17, 3.25, 3.4) suggests the survey data follows a near-symmetrical distribution, increasing confidence in the reliability of findings. This validates the primary data collection methodology and confirms that oil price shocks from events like the 2026 Iran conflict are felt widely across households in Betul. (Al-Ghais, OPEC 2023)
